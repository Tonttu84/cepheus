# Decisions

One row per decision. Never rewrite an old row: a reversal is a new row that names the one it replaces.

| ID | Decision | Reason | Chosen against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Start in a local repository with commits straight to `main` and no remote | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "lets create a local repo, my friend will create the team repo ... we can later add it to the team repo". History stays organised before the team repository exists | Waiting for the team repository (work would sit uncommitted); a branch and pull-request flow (there is no remote to open a pull request against) |
| D2 | Install the commit hooks for this clone only (`install.sh --git-hooks`), not as a committed `.githooks/` | This history will later be added to a team repository the owner does not own; committed hooks would impose this workflow on it | The installer's default mode, which commits `.githooks/` and a `.gitattributes` rule |
| D3 | Keep `.private/` and `.watch/` untracked | They hold private notes and event credit codes; history that is pushed later cannot be cleanly scrubbed | Tracking everything in the folder |
| D4 | Track the planning material in `docs/`: the general plan, the research reports and the doctor's notes, with no names and no message times. Replaces D3 for the planning files; the watch files and the raw notes stay untracked | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "We can put it out as long as it is anonymous. It isnt really that sensitive, it helps other AI understand the context? Though we could maybe format them better" | Keeping the doctor's notes untracked, which D3 did on the grounds that pushed history cannot be scrubbed |
| D5 | Changes reach `main` through a branch and a review. The first push of the planning documents went straight to `main` as a one-off. Replaces the "commits straight to `main`" part of D1 | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "Lets do it through PRs and reviews etc generally. We can push this time But lets not do it again" | Continuing to commit straight to `main`, which suited a local repository with one author but not a shared one |
| D6 | Reviews run as Entire trails: a branch is published with `entire trail create`, marked ready, reviewed, and merged with `entire trail merge` | The team repository is hosted on Entire, where a trail is the pull request, and the owner wants the team to learn its agent features (13 Nov 2026: "we should try to take benefit from it in the workflow") | Plain branches with reviews agreed in chat, which leave no record next to the change; pull requests on GitHub, which would need the repository mirrored there |
| D7 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 3). The core of the product is a flow: ask short questions; warn when the answers move away from the person's baseline and stay there for two rounds, or when two rounds go unanswered; then a nurse calls. The ring and the camera only support it | Questionnaires are far better validated than any sensor we looked at, the flow is cheap, and a call sorts out false alarms. The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "The flow idea looks good, wait until a warning is triggered then a nurse calls" | A sensor-led system; a nurse or an AI asking questions every week |
| D8 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 5). The response is split by recipient: a small dip goes to the patient as one simple suggestion; a change that stays, or silence, goes to the clinic as a flag naming the broad area that changed, never the raw data | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: a depressed patient needs something simple, the healthcare system does not; and "the doctor only gets the info that flag has been raised, not what raised the flag". Research on alerts found that a flag with no reason at all is ignored, so the flag names the area | Sending the clinic the measures themselves; sending a flag with no reason |
| D9 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 4). Questions come twice a week with the PHQ-9 every two weeks, and there is no daily tier. Anything about self-harm goes to the crisis path at once, and the crisis path is always in view | A relapse builds over weeks, so about a week of delay is acceptable, and daily questions wear people out. The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "If a delay of a week is not big deal then why do we even need a daily option" | Questions every morning; a stepped schedule that turns daily when something moves |
| D10 | Owner's decision, open to the team (side-challenge notes, question 1). Nothing is built only to enter a side challenge. The team enters MLH's "Best Use of Entire", which matches how it already works, and no other for now | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "Our focus is to do a good system to actually detect depression and maybe treat it ... we dont add anything just to add AI for side challenges" | Building a Gemini feature for the Google challenge; entering Modal or Verda, which need a model we serve ourselves, or Gemma, when no task in the plan needs a model of our own |
| D11 | Owner's decision, open to the team. A signal that has not been studied may be used if the system can verify it: every reading is stored next to what happened afterwards. Unverified assumptions are listed in the plan with their risks | The Aalto mentors' view, relayed by the owner on 13 Nov 2026: something not studied is fine if there is a way to verify it, by a study or by collecting data. The owner: "make slightly positive assumptions but note down the difficulty" | Using only signals with published evidence; using untested signals with no way to check them |
| D12 | Owner's decision, open to the team. A home or money signal never raises a flag alone: it must agree with the patient's own answers and last a week or more | The research found no evidence that such signals predict a relapse, and a single weak signal would give mostly false flags (`docs/research/7-behaviour-flags.md`, on an open trail when this row was written) | Letting any signal raise a flag; leaving home and money signals out altogether |
| D13 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 6 covers one part). The patient switches each method of measuring on separately, sees what it records, and can switch it off again | Some methods are intrusive, and the owner's condition for them is that the patient agrees (13 Nov 2026: "the patient would need to approve the methods used too as they are intrusive") | One consent for everything; methods the patient cannot see or switch off |
| D14 | Owner's decision, open to the team. We build for real detection, and we say only that the system supports the clinic's judgment about when to act. The product makes no claim to detect, predict, diagnose or treat | A claim to detect or predict relapse makes software a medical device in the EU, and the evidence for any single signal is early | Presenting the product as relapse detection; avoiding the aim itself |
| D15 | Owner's decision, open to the team. One TypeScript web app: Next.js with strict TypeScript and Tailwind, SQLite through Prisma, Zod, MediaPipe in the browser, the signal maths in TypeScript, Vitest and Playwright | The camera analysis runs in the browser, so video never leaves the device; one language and one thing to run suits several people and their agents working at once; the maths is small | A Python service for the calculations beside a web front end |
| D16 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 11 covers the clinic's part). After a warning, the nurse's call chooses among steps: simple things and a medication check for a small dip, a booster cluster of about 5 TMS sessions when the decline holds, a new course for a full relapse, urgent care for a crisis. The system never recommends a treatment | TMS is safe and re-treatment works for earlier responders, but clinic capacity is scarce, so boosters should go to those who need them; the simple things cost nothing but are too weak for a real relapse. The owner agreed to this on 14 Nov 2026 ("Makes sense") | Re-treating at every warning; offering only self-help; letting the software choose the step |
| D17 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Data kept centrally in the EU; the phone is only a screen and keeps nothing but a sign-in cookie | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "either safely on the phone or then it needs to be stored in EU, I imagine we would benefit from collecting the data so store it" | Keeping data on the phone (nothing to check the rule against) |
| D18 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The product's hosting target is a managed PostgreSQL in Helsinki from a Finnish-owned provider; until an account or credits exist, hosting is simulated (production mode against a real PostgreSQL) | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "Or we at least simulate the hosting" and "We can aim to host properly if we get credits" | A US-owned provider's EU region; paying before there is anything to host |
| D19 | Owner's decision, open to the team (trail 19, now on main). PostgreSQL replaces the SQLite part of D15; the rest of D15 stays | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "PostgreSQL is fine" | SQLite as a file beside the app |
| D20 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Patients are pseudonymous: a random code, no name, phone number, email, date of birth or identity number in our database; the clinic keeps the link; clinic staff are named accounts | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "Pseudonymous" | Names and contact details next to the answers |
| D21 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The patient agrees in three separate layers: taking part; each extra measuring method (D13); use of pseudonymous answers and outcomes to check the rule, off until switched on | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "Yes, separate" | One agreement for everything |
| D22 | Owner's decision, open to the team (written into docs/questions.md on trail 10; carried out in the rule now on main). A first complete PHQ-9 of 10 or more raises a warning at once and stores no baseline | A patient whose very first score is high needs a nurse's call as much as one who relapses later. The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "makes sense" | Only telling the clinic that the person is not at a well baseline |
| D23 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Effort is not used as an extra signal: nothing effortful is asked on purpose to read effort from it | Anything asked of an unwell person costs almost no effort (AGENTS.md), and the research found the effort deficit small. The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "lets drop the effort as extra metric" | A deliberate multi-step task as a signal |
| D24 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The product is presented as an app version of the tested model of following patients up with questions and nurse calls; everything beyond that core is an improvement marked as experimental | From the owner's report of the Aalto mentors' view, 14 Nov 2026: "the solutions doesnt need to be super smart if there is already a tested working system" | Presenting a new detection method as the core |
| D25 | Owner's decision, open to the team. An experimental rotating extra question: at most one extra single question per round, rotating through a small set on the 0 to 4 scale, skippable at no cost, off by default, never used by the warning rule, the baselines or the flags, and marked experimental. Replaces, for this one switchable extra only, the part of the question set's rule that nothing outside the curated set is asked | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "it adds at least some potential improvement, we can mark it experimental in the submission and it is easy to remove if it doesnt work out. It is very modular and not load bearing if it works with the simple set" | Rotating the core questions, which would break the comparison between rounds; asking nothing more |
| D26 | Owner's decision, open to the team (trail 21, proposal 3). A patient can always send the clinic a message about something the questions did not ask; a person reads every message; the crisis path and a sentence on urgent help are always beside it. Replaces, for this message only, the question set's "No free text in the must-have build" (docs/questions.md, trail 10) | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "we absolutely should have the option to tell something that was not asked" and "it is kind of safety net that we didnt ignore a patient that wanted some help" | No free text; a contact-me button only |
| D27 | Owner's decision, open to the team. For those messages only, a model running on our own server may mark a message as possibly urgent. It can only raise a message, never lower, hide, delay or summarise one; every message is still read by a person in the order it arrived. For this mark only, this sets aside the AGENTS.md rule that a general AI model must not decide when to alert a clinician; every other rule still applies. The server's safety is flagged as a concern to settle before real use | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "We could scan it with AI for priority. It is just an additional safety layer", "It is fine, we override the rule for this", and "We just need to decide that the server needs to be safe, flag it as concern, this is just a demo not the end product" | No AI near the messages; an AI that sorts or summarises messages |
| D28 | Owner's decision, open to the team (trail 22). Where a teammate's open trail overlaps the owner's or his agents' work, the teammate's goes first unless it is clearly worse, and the owner's side builds on top of it | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "If his trail 22 isnt clearly worse we can go with his choices, I have generated most of the code so it would be polite to go with theirs when there is an overlap" | Merging the owner's own version first |
| D29 | Owner's decision, open to the team. On Linux, teammates install the system's PostgreSQL client library themselves for the packaged local database; it is noted in AGENTS.md (now on main) | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "They can install postgres themselves" | A hosted development database for everyone |
| D30 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 8). The claim that people answer a machine more honestly than a person is used only as a hypothesis, in the weaker form the sources support (sometimes more willing to report sensitive things to a screen than to an interviewer, mainly when alone), unless a stronger source is found | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "we will keep is a hypothesis if we cant find a source" | Stating it as fact |
| D31 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The product raises a flag for possible relapse risk and passes it to a nurse or doctor, who makes the judgement. It makes no diagnosis and no treatment claim, and it never tells the patient that it has detected or predicted a relapse. Sensing is described as a wellbeing check-in. Never claim brain measurement or brain stimulation at home. Replaces the old AGENTS.md rule (first bullet of "Rules of the problem") and decision D14 | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: flagging possible relapse risk for clinicians is the purpose of the product and of the challenge it answers. The product does not make the final judgement; that is passed to nurses and doctors | The old rule forbidding any claim of detecting or predicting relapse; claiming detection directly to patients; letting software make the diagnostic judgement |
| D32 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Project rules are a baseline: a rule may be relaxed for a real payoff on the owner's word, as a separate, switchable module, with a decision row naming the rule it relaxes | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: project rules are a baseline and may be relaxed for a real payoff if isolated in a separate, switchable module and documented with a decision row naming the rule it relaxes | Treating project rules as immutable regardless of payoff; unmodular or undocumented rule relaxation |
| D33 | Owner's decision, open to the team (as part of the proposal for a message read by a person). An optional free-text question is worded generally, "Is there something else you want to tell us?", and is optional | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: every free-text answer needs a person to read it, which would use up the benefit of automation if it were asked about health every round; a health-specific open question is added only if evidence shows a benefit | A mandatory free-text question; asking an open health-specific question every round without evidence of benefit |
| D34 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Patients are identified with Finland's official strong electronic identification. For the hackathon the identification service is simulated behind an interface, so that a real one can replace it. This answers the build plan's open question of whether an enrolment code is enough, and replaces the part of build-plan row B7 that says there is no sign-in through other services | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: a service that shows a person their health data should know who the person is with the country's standard means | An enrolment code alone |
| D35 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Other conditions are piloted with one: anxiety, measured with the GAD-7 questionnaire, switched on only for patients who have it. It is a complete working feature that raises warnings in the same way as the PHQ-9, using the questionnaire's own published scoring and cut-off. The demonstration has two simulated sample patients, one with depression only and one with depression and anxiety | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: both conditions need a working use case, and a validated questionnaire means we do not have to invent the rule | Storing GAD-7 answers without warnings; an ad-hoc anxiety question |
| D36 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The PHQ-9 and the GAD-7 use default thresholds from their published cut-offs, held in the rule's configuration, where they can be changed. No threshold is set or learned for one person; personal customisation (weights or thresholds from how a person answers when well, or set by the clinic) is described as a future improvement and is not built. The personal baseline already built for the two twice-weekly taps stays, since the taps have no published cut-off | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: the defaults are enough to show the product working, and warnings must still make sense for both sample patients | A per-patient threshold now |
| D37 | Owner's decision, open to the team. A small reward for answering, with a rewarded walk or an activity the person chooses on other days, stays in the proposal. The legal difficulties of rewarding patients are written down, and sponsoring is explored as a route; the proposal stands even if the law would have to change before it could be used | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: a few euros a week is small against the cost of care, and answering is what the whole product depends on | Dropping the idea because of the legal limits |
| D38 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The rewarded walk shows the person a visible count of their walks. This relaxes the rule "No streaks, no scores" in AGENTS.md for the walk only, under D32: it is a separate module that can be switched off, the count is never used for the question rounds, and a missed answer is still recorded as missed and never punished | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: a count seems to encourage people to keep up the activity | No count |
| D39 | Owner's decision, open to the team. If patients' free-text messages get an AI mark, the mark can only raise a message's priority. It never lowers, hides or sorts out a message, and a person at the clinic still reads every message | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: the mark may help an urgent message be read sooner, and must never be the reason one is read later | Any AI sorting that could delay a message |
| D40 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The bedside voice unit (a small Linux board that speaks the two check-in questions and maps the spoken answer to a fixed choice) is part of the demonstration and goes into `main` once its review findings are fixed | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "Yes, include it"; it also opens an avenue for more signals (eye movement, expression, voice) to pursue | Keeping the hardware exploration outside the product |
| D41 | Owner's decision, open to the team. Camera and microphone input stays processed on the device and only derived numbers are stored, never audio or pictures. Richer derived numbers (eye movement, expression, timing of speech) may be added, and machine learning on those numbers is presented as an avenue to pursue, with no claim that it works | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: storing recordings only matters once the data is actually used, and the potential can be kept without holding recordings of patients' faces and voices | Storing raw audio or pictures now for later machine learning |
| D42 | Owner's decision, open to the team. The status page may also show a visible count or streak for answering the question rounds, beside the count of walks (D38). This relaxes the rule "No streaks, no scores" in `AGENTS.md` for this display only, under D32: it can be switched on or off, it is never used by the warning rule, the baselines or the flags, and a missed answer is still recorded as missed and never punished | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "Yes we can add to both walking and questions but make it so that it can be turned on or off" | No count for the question rounds; a count that cannot be switched off |
| D43 | Owner's decision, open to the team. There is one API for the product. A new way of answering (the bedside voice unit, a phone, a later app) calls the same check-in routes and the same use cases, with its own way of proving who or what is calling; it does not get its own routes with their own storage. A separate service is added only when there is a real reason to split | The owner, 14 Nov 2026: "we should not have multiple parallel APIs unless we really need to split services for some reason" | A second check-in route with its own storage for each device |
