# Decisions

One row per decision. Never rewrite an old row: a reversal is a new row that names the one it replaces.

| ID | Decision | Reason | Chosen against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Start in a local repository with commits straight to `main` and no remote | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "lets create a local repo, my friend will create the team repo ... we can later add it to the team repo". History stays organised before the team repository exists | Waiting for the team repository (work would sit uncommitted); a branch and pull-request flow (there is no remote to open a pull request against) |
| D2 | Install the commit hooks for this clone only (`install.sh --git-hooks`), not as a committed `.githooks/` | This history will later be added to a team repository the owner does not own; committed hooks would impose this workflow on it | The installer's default mode, which commits `.githooks/` and a `.gitattributes` rule |
| D3 | Keep `.private/` and `.watch/` untracked | They hold private notes and event credit codes; history that is pushed later cannot be cleanly scrubbed | Tracking everything in the folder |
| D4 | Track the planning material in `docs/`: the general plan, the research reports and the doctor's notes, with no names and no message times. Replaces D3 for the planning files; the watch files and the raw notes stay untracked | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "We can put it out as long as it is anonymous. It isnt really that sensitive, it helps other AI understand the context? Though we could maybe format them better" | Keeping the doctor's notes untracked, which D3 did on the grounds that pushed history cannot be scrubbed |
| D5 | Changes reach `main` through a branch and a review. The first push of the planning documents went straight to `main` as a one-off. Replaces the "commits straight to `main`" part of D1 | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "Lets do it through PRs and reviews etc generally. We can push this time But lets not do it again" | Continuing to commit straight to `main`, which suited a local repository with one author but not a shared one |
| D6 | Reviews run as Entire trails: a branch is published with `entire trail create`, marked ready, reviewed, and merged with `entire trail merge` | The team repository is hosted on Entire, where a trail is the pull request, and the owner wants the team to learn its agent features (13 Nov 2026: "we should try to take benefit from it in the workflow") | Plain branches with reviews agreed in chat, which leave no record next to the change; pull requests on GitHub, which would need the repository mirrored there |
| D7 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 3). The core of the product is a flow: ask short questions; warn when the answers move away from the person's baseline and stay there for two rounds, or when two rounds go unanswered; then a nurse calls. The ring and the camera only support it | Questionnaires are far better validated than any sensor we looked at, the flow is cheap, and a call sorts out false alarms. The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "The flow idea looks good, wait until a warning is triggered then a nurse calls" | A sensor-led system; a nurse or an AI asking questions every week |
| D8 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 5). The response is split by recipient: a small dip goes to the patient as one simple suggestion; a change that stays, or silence, goes to the clinic as a flag naming the broad area that changed, never the raw data | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: a depressed patient needs something simple, the healthcare system does not; and "the doctor only gets the info that flag has been raised, not what raised the flag". Research on alerts found that a flag with no reason at all is ignored, so the flag names the area | Sending the clinic the measures themselves; sending a flag with no reason |
| D9 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 4). Questions come twice a week with the PHQ-9 every two weeks, and there is no daily tier. Anything about self-harm goes to the crisis path at once, and the crisis path is always in view | A relapse builds over weeks, so about a week of delay is acceptable, and daily questions wear people out. The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "If a delay of a week is not big deal then why do we even need a daily option" | Questions every morning; a stepped schedule that turns daily when something moves |
| D10 | Owner's decision, open to the team (side-challenge notes, question 1). Nothing is built only to enter a side challenge. The team enters MLH's "Best Use of Entire", which matches how it already works, and no other for now | The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "Our focus is to do a good system to actually detect depression and maybe treat it ... we dont add anything just to add AI for side challenges" | Building a Gemini feature for the Google challenge; entering Modal or Verda, which need a model we serve ourselves, or Gemma, when no task in the plan needs a model of our own |
| D11 | Owner's decision, open to the team. A signal that has not been studied may be used if the system can verify it: every reading is stored next to what happened afterwards. Unverified assumptions are listed in the plan with their risks | The Aalto mentors' view, relayed by the owner on 13 Nov 2026: something not studied is fine if there is a way to verify it, by a study or by collecting data. The owner: "make slightly positive assumptions but note down the difficulty" | Using only signals with published evidence; using untested signals with no way to check them |
| D12 | Owner's decision, open to the team. A home or money signal never raises a flag alone: it must agree with the patient's own answers and last a week or more | The research found no evidence that such signals predict a relapse, and a single weak signal would give mostly false flags (`docs/research/7-behaviour-flags.md`, on an open trail when this row was written) | Letting any signal raise a flag; leaving home and money signals out altogether |
| D13 | Owner's decision, open to the team (plan question 6 covers one part). The patient switches each method of measuring on separately, sees what it records, and can switch it off again | Some methods are intrusive, and the owner's condition for them is that the patient agrees (13 Nov 2026: "the patient would need to approve the methods used too as they are intrusive") | One consent for everything; methods the patient cannot see or switch off |
| D14 | Owner's decision, open to the team. We build for real detection, and we say only that the system supports the clinic's judgment about when to act. The product makes no claim to detect, predict, diagnose or treat | A claim to detect or predict relapse makes software a medical device in the EU, and the evidence for any single signal is early | Presenting the product as relapse detection; avoiding the aim itself |
| D15 | Owner's decision, open to the team. One TypeScript web app: Next.js with strict TypeScript and Tailwind, SQLite through Prisma, Zod, MediaPipe in the browser, the signal maths in TypeScript, Vitest and Playwright | The camera analysis runs in the browser, so video never leaves the device; one language and one thing to run suits several people and their agents working at once; the maths is small | A Python service for the calculations beside a web front end |
