# Existing products for depression at home: what failed, and where the gap is

Run date: 2026-11-13. This builds on `evidence-check.md` (its "Products named in the brief" section covers Meru Health's RCT and Soihtu's profile, and section 13 covers streaks, orthosomnia and self-monitoring; neither is repeated here).

How the sources were read: **(read)** means the page itself was fetched and read in this run. **(search only)** means only the search engine's abstract or snippets were seen, because the page refused automated fetches (PubMed, STAT paywall, JMIR PDF) or there was no time. Marketing claims are marked as such.

---

## Where the gap is (summary)

1. **Monitoring without a response failed; responses without a pathway failed.** Mindstrong built a passive "smoke alarm" on thin evidence and ended up as text therapy before closing (2023). Pear, Akili and Woebot had cleared or near-cleared treatments but no one to pay for them, or could not afford the regulatory route (2023 to 2025). Nothing found joins a *personal* early-warning signal to an *already proven personal* response and to a clinic that can act (for TMS: a booster or maintenance session).
2. **Relapse prevention after remission is still at the protocol stage.** StayFine (Utrecht, NCT05551468) and a 2025 JMIR early-warning app study exist, but no completed RCT of an app used after depression treatment was found, and no product aimed at the period after TMS (the evidence check also found none).
3. **Personal learning of what helps exists only in research or in other domains.** MyBehavior (bandit for physical activity), MOSS (adapts to preferences), Cue (human-written nudges from sensed rhythms). None learns, while the person is well, which sensory or behavioural response shifts *their* state and then offers it at a dip.
4. So the defensible novelty is the **combination and the timing**: experiments while well, rehearsal, one-tap delivery when unwell, and a clinician view tied to a concrete clinical action, not any single component.

## A. The landscape

### A1. Prescription digital therapeutics (PDTs) for depression

- **Rejoyn (Otsuka / Click Therapeutics, CT-152).** A 6-week app of "Emotional Faces Memory Task" cognitive-emotional training plus short lessons, as an adjunct for adults on antidepressants. FDA cleared on 1 April 2024 via 510(k). Pivotal Mirai RCT: 386 people, Rejoyn versus sham app; the company's release reports improvement from baseline but not the between-group comparison, and press coverage reports the primary difference was not statistically significant (scores 19 versus 21). The label says the app does not monitor symptoms or alert prescribers. Status: active (2024). Model: prescription.
  - https://www.otsuka-us.com/news/rejoyn-fda-authorized (read; company release)
  - https://abc7news.com/depression-disorder-digital-treatment-rejoyn-mobile-app-fda-approval/14610421/ (search only; non-significant primary result, Torous's caution)
- **deprexis (Germany).** Self-help programme for depression; the manufacturer says it is permanently listed as a DiGA (reimbursed by German statutory insurance) and cites 16 trials (marketing claim). Permanent DiGA listing requires an RCT showing benefit versus usual care. https://deprexis.com/de-en/overview-for-healthcare-professionals (search only); https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12913-023-09287-w (search only)
- **DiGA delistings in general.** A 2025 npj Digital Medicine paper reports 11 more DiGAs delisted: six because no positive healthcare effect could be shown, one because its study could not be completed, four at the manufacturer's request. Which were depression apps was not visible. https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41746-025-01879-6 (search only)
- **Big Health (SleepioRx, DaylightRx).** Not depression, but the closest commercial success: FDA-cleared in August and September 2024 for insomnia and generalised anxiety, and payable under new Medicare digital mental health codes from 1 January 2025; a February 2026 funding round of $23.7M was reported. Efficacy figures (76% and 71% "clinical improvement") are company-reported. https://www.biospace.com/fda/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx (search only)
- **Pear Therapeutics (reSET, reSET-O, Somryst).** First FDA-authorised PDT (2017). Filed Chapter 11 on 7 April 2023; CEO blamed payers who deny payment for effective, cost-saving therapies, and the economic climate. Earlier cut 22% of staff. Assets auctioned in May 2023 for about $6M (search only). Closed.
  - https://pharmaphorum.com/news/pear-tx-files-bankruptcy-will-seek-sell-assets (read)
  - https://invivo.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/IV147722/Pear-Bankruptcy-Filing-Highlights-Reimbursement-Barriers-for-Digital-Therapeutics (search only; coverage in about 10 states and few payers, Medicare did not cover PDTs as of 2022)

### A2. App programmes with coaching or therapy

- **Meru Health.** See the evidence check. Status: active; a July 2025 partnership with Harvard Pilgrim Health Care was reported; no equity round found since its 2021 $38M Series B (third-party trackers, search only). Model: sold to health plans and employers. https://www.cbinsights.com/company/meru-health (search only)
- **Woebot Health.** CBT chatbot; retired its consumer app on 30 June 2025. The founder told STAT the shutdown was largely due to the cost and difficulty of FDA marketing authorisation, and that the FDA had not worked out how to regulate LLM-based tools. Pivoted (details paywalled). https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-mental-health-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/ (read, opening only)
- **Cue (HealthRhythms).** Passive sensing (steps, screen, GPS, sleep timing) plus daily mood, a clinician dashboard, and micro-interventions every 2 to 3 days based on the last 3 to 4 days of data (written by hand by the study team). 16-week RCT, 133 outpatients, versus monitoring only: no significant slope difference overall (d = −0.10); in 28 people depressed at entry d = −0.72, p = 0.059. Authors linked to the company. Frontiers in Digital Health 2022. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.870522/full (read)

### A3. Game-based treatments

- **Soihtu DTx / Meliora.** The evidence check found no results. New in this run: an Aalto University trial of "MEL-T01, Meliora" (NCT05426265), three arms, 12 weeks, PHQ-9 primary outcome, ran June 2022 to August 2024, listed as completed, last updated January 2025. No published results found. The registry does not name Soihtu, so the link is likely but unconfirmed. https://www.centerwatch.com/clinical-trials/listings/NCT05426265/the-effects-of-videogames-on-depression-symptoms-and-brain-dynamics (search only)
- **Akili (EndeavorRx).** FDA-cleared ADHD game, not depression, but the key game-PDT business case. Sold to Virtual Therapeutics for about $34M (announced 30 May 2024) after a 2022 listing valued at about $1B. EndeavorRx "struggled to find payers"; Akili moved to over-the-counter sales in 2023; 2023 revenue $1.7M against a $59.5M loss. Closed as a standalone company. https://www.medtechdive.com/news/akili-sell-34m-virtual-therapeutics-digital/717570/ (read)
- **Evidence for games and cognitive training on mood** is weak (evidence check section 7: Motter 2016; Legemaat 2022).
- **Smartphone attention-bias modification** (JMIR Mental Health 2024; 12 papers, 24,503 participants): active training g = −0.18 on symptoms, placebo training g = −0.38; only active training reduced the bias itself. In the depression subgroup, active and placebo were comparable and non-significant; gamified versus non-gamified made no difference. Authors: effect on depression "appears limited". https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11406109/ (read)

### A4. Passive sensing and digital phenotyping companies

- **Mindstrong Health.** Raised about $160M on a digital "smoke alarm" (smartphone interaction biomarkers). Built on a 2018 pilot of 27 people over 7 days (search only). Scaled back to largely text-based therapy, then disclosed layoffs in February 2023 and stopped treating patients in March 2023; SonderMind bought the technology. STAT: tension between slow validation and startup pace; engineers and clinicians disputed whether the technology was ready; Insel: the idea "might have been right" but the execution was not. Closed (2023).
  - https://www.statnews.com/2023/02/28/mindstrong-mental-health-technology-prediction/ (read, opening only)
  - https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/mindstrong-sells-tech-assets-sondermind-shuts-down-operations (search only; 10 March 2023 end of patient services, ~100 jobs lost)
- **Thymia (London).** Game-like tasks that analyse voice, video and behaviour; a wellness line (Helios) and a clinical line (Apollo, described as investigational). Claims up to 84% accuracy (company claim). Status 2025 unclear; no regulatory clearance found. https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/thymia-raises-2-1m-for-game-inspired-mental-health-ai/ (search only)
- **Research context** (accuracy, RADAR-MDD) is in the evidence check section 5.

### A5. Voice biomarkers

- **Kintsugi.** AI that flags depression and anxiety from speech; raised about $30M. Closed commercial operations on 11 February 2026 and open-sourced its models and research. Founder: chose "the integrity of the science over the limitations of a distressed market"; other coverage says it could not obtain timely FDA De Novo clearance (search only). Closed. https://bhbusiness.com/2026/02/11/mental-health-voice-biomarker-kintsugi-closes-makes-all-technology-and-research-public/ (read)
- **Ellipsis Health.** Voice-based screening for depression and anxiety; a later headline reports a $45M raise for an "AI care manager", suggesting a shift away from pure biomarkers (not confirmed). https://insights.citeline.com/medtech-insight/digital-technologies/ai/ellipsis-raises-45m-to-expand-ai-care-manager-across-health-systems-WNPVPGQDPNFPHODM2XCILINY4I/link-34G2TNYDVRAO7K55TAEDHFBSJU (search only)
- **Sonde Health.** Voice health monitoring; launched a "cognitive fitness" product. Current status: no source found.

### A6. Consumer wearables and phones

- **Apple Health (iOS 17, 2023).** "State of Mind" mood logging and the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 questionnaires, exportable as a PDF for a clinician; validated only in some countries and languages. Status: active. https://support.apple.com/en-mide/105070 (search only)
- **Fitbit / Google.** Stress Management Score (0 to 100), mood logging after "body response" notifications, mindfulness behind a subscription. https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/fitbit/manage-stress-fitbit/ (search only)
- **Oura.** Daytime Stress, Resilience (two-week balance of stress and recovery), Cumulative Stress; consolidated into a stress dashboard in October 2025. https://www.wareable.com/health-and-wellbeing/stress-research-wearables-8887 (search only)
- **Samsung Galaxy Watch.** Stress score including electrodermal activity. Same source (search only).
- None of these claims to detect depression or relapse; they stay on the wellness side of the line (see E).

### A7. Home neuromodulation

- **Flow Neuroscience (tDCS headset + app).** FDA approval (PMA) reported for December 2025 as the first home brain-stimulation device for depression; CE class IIa in Europe since 2019. Pivotal RCT (Nature Medicine): 174 adults, 10 weeks, home use under remote supervision; HDRS improved 9.41 versus 7.14 points for sham; 58% remission reported for active. US launch expected 2026, prescription required. https://patientcareonline.com/view/fda-clears-first-at-home-brain-stimulation-device-for-major-depression (read); https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41475514/ (search only)
- **Sooma (Finland).** Home tDCS prescribed through public healthcare in Finland and Estonia; company says over 25,000 patients treated (claim). Evidence: a retrospective Finnish cohort (22.4% mean improvement, no control); a US pivotal trial of about 200 people announced March 2025, results not found. https://sifted.eu/articles/sooma-raise-depression-treatment (search only); https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2025/03/12/lindus-health-and-sooma-medical-announce-pivotal-device-clinical-trial-for-treatment-of-mdd (search only)
- Relevance: a home stimulation device plus app is the nearest product to "stay well after TMS", but it is a treatment, not a learn-what-helps or early-warning loop.

### A8. Relapse prevention and staying well after treatment

- **StayFine (Utrecht).** RCT of 254 people aged 13 to 21 in remission of depression or anxiety: app monitoring plus guided, personalised modules (three of six chosen from symptom networks and shared decisions with an expert by experience) versus monitoring alone, relapse over 36 months. Protocol only; no results found. https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/personalised-app-based-relapse-prevention-of-depressive-and-anxie/ ; https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1229713 (search only)
- **Early-warning app with personal ML (JMIR mHealth 2025, e67141).** Focus groups with people in remission about an app combining questionnaires and phone and watch data into a personal early-warning model. https://mhealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e67141 (search only; page did not load)
- **Personal warning signals (qualitative, 66 people).** About 65% of the 162 warning signals people named were *not* DSM depression symptoms (irritability, rumination, withdrawing, struggling with time). Monitoring a fixed symptom list misses most of what people notice. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9471442/ (read)
- **Other registered trials:** a 6-week daily-monitoring app (NCT03678194), SMARD smartphone cognitive training for recurrence; no results found. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03678194 (search only)
- No product for the period after TMS was found (consistent with the evidence check section 8).

### A9. Clinician-side remote monitoring and measurement-based care

- **Greenspace Health.** Measurement-based care platform: clients fill questionnaires between sessions; clinician dashboard flags suicidal ideation, deterioration, lack of improvement, low engagement and low alliance ("Caseload Assistant"). Company material only; no independent evaluation found. https://greenspacehealth.com/en-ca/measurement-based-care/ (search only)
- **Cue** dashboard (A2) and **Apple Health PDF export** (A6) are other examples.
- Evidence that measurement-based care improves depression outcomes: not checked in this run.

## B. Engagement reality

- **Real-world apps (Baumel et al., JMIR 2019;21(9):e14567).** Independent panel usage data: median 15-day retention 3.9% and 30-day retention 3.3% across mental-health apps; peer-support, mindfulness and tracker apps retained better than breathing apps (median 0%). https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567 (search only; the page and PDF returned empty)
- **Trials (Torous et al., J Affect Disord 2020).** 18 RCTs of depression apps, 3,336 people: pooled dropout 26.2%, 47.8% after adjusting for publication bias. Dropout was lower with **human feedback (11.7% versus 34%)** and with **in-app mood monitoring (18.4% versus 37.9%)**. https://www.nationalelfservice.net/treatment/digital-health/dropout-rate-depression-apps/ (read; summary of the paper)
- **Research cohorts** reach ~80% questionnaire compliance (evidence check section 6); that is the paid-study ceiling, not real-world use.
- **What improves engagement (Borghouts et al., JMIR 2021; 208 articles).** Facilitators: social connectedness, insight into one's health, sense of control. Barriers: severe symptoms, technical problems, lack of personalisation. https://www.jmir.org/2021/3/e24387 (search only)
- **What people with depression say (BJPsych Open 2025, meta-synthesis of 14 studies).** They want a simple, intuitive and *passive* protocol, personalisation and control over their own data; many reported that mood monitoring *worsened* mood and anxiety. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12241945/ (read)
- **Patients in a focus-group study** wanted information about depression, self-monitoring and help in an emergency. https://doaj.org/article/7fef758dae0a4221b5f85dacbb7773be (search only)
- **Missing prompts track state.** In young adults, higher stress and negative affect at one prompt predicted missing the next one. https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/204987 (search only)

## C. Failures and their causes

| Case | Year | What it was | Reported cause | Category |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mindstrong | 2023 | Passive phone biomarkers, then care | Thin validation (27-person pilot), commercialised too early, clinicians doubted the predictions, pivot to text therapy | Weak signal; no clinical pathway; investor pace |
| Pear Therapeutics | 2023 | FDA-authorised PDTs (addiction, insomnia) | Payers would not pay; low coverage; economic climate | Reimbursement |
| Akili | 2024 | FDA-cleared game (ADHD) | No payers; OTC pivot earned $1.2M; $59.5M loss | Reimbursement; business model |
| Woebot | 2025 | CBT chatbot | Cost of FDA authorisation; no route for LLM tools | Regulation |
| Kintsugi | 2026 | Voice biomarker | No timely FDA De Novo clearance; "distressed market" | Regulation; market |
| Rejoyn | 2024 (active) | Emotional-faces training PDT | Cleared, but primary endpoint versus sham not significant; payment unclear | Weak effect; reimbursement risk |
| DiGA delistings | to 2025 | German reimbursed apps | Six could not show a healthcare effect | Evidence |

Sources as in section A. Privacy: Mindstrong was criticised for lack of transparency with patients about data collection (search only, STAT summary); no other privacy-driven failure was found.

**Lessons.** (1) A signal is worth nothing unless someone acts on it; Mindstrong's alarm had no proven action behind it. (2) Prescription status without a payer kills companies (Pear, Akili); Big Health only became viable once Medicare codes existed. (3) Diagnostic or predictive claims trigger the full regulatory route (Kintsugi, Woebot), which small companies could not fund.

## D. Prior art for the six ideas

| # | Idea | Closest existing thing | Verdict | What would still be different |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Train while well, one tap when unwell | StayFine (personalised modules chosen in remission, protocol); MBCT-style relapse-prevention plans; N-of-1 self-experiment platforms (MINEs / HackYourHealth, NCT03695263; Frontiers Public Health 2020 30-day self-experiments for stress and happiness) | **Partly done** | Nobody found runs short *experiments* while well to measure which response shifts this person's state, then rehearses it and serves it with one tap at a detected dip. StayFine picks modules by assessment, not by measured effect. |
| 2 | Game measuring negativity bias as early sign | Research: Bouhuys 1999 (23 remitted, negative face reading predicted relapse); Ruhe 2019 (69 remitted, 2.5 years: misclassifying faces as angry linked to time to recurrence, but demographics and childhood adversity predicted best). Products: P1vital PReDicT test (faces task for antidepressant choice; 913-person RCT negative on primary outcome); Thymia (games for measurement); Rejoyn (faces task as *treatment*) | **Partly done** | No product found uses repeated home face-reading as a *personal-baseline relapse signal*. Caveats: small studies, practice effects, attention-bias training shows placebo-sized effects. |
| 3 | Missed or slow answer as a signal | Research only: stress and negative affect predict missing the next EMA prompt (Lancaster); response-time "paradata" studies (JMIR mHealth 2023 e45203; JMIR Mental Health 2024, drift rate r = −0.27 with depression); transformer predicting non-response (AUC 0.77, arXiv 2111.01193) | **Partly done (research), new as a product feature** | No product found treats non-response or latency as a relapse marker. Must separate "busy" from "unwell" (personal baseline, context). |
| 4 | Per-person learning of which response works | MyBehavior (multi-armed bandit, physical activity and diet; small RCTs, 2015); MOSS (ETH, 126 adults, adapts CBT suggestions to time, place and preference, 2016); a 2025 arXiv study using Thompson sampling for adaptive mental-health prompts; Cue (human-curated) | **Partly done** | Not found for sensory or social responses (music, light, movement, contact) in depression, nor learned during remission and reused at a dip. |
| 5 | Clinician view of who is slipping | Greenspace Health (deterioration and risk flags), Mindstrong (original model), Cue dashboard, Apple Health PDF export | **Already done** | Difference only in context: a TMS clinic view tied to one concrete action (book a booster session), fed by passive plus minimal active data rather than questionnaires. |
| 6 | Forgiving design: no streaks, show only what is actionable | Supported as a direction by the 2025 meta-synthesis (monitoring can worsen mood; users want simple and passive) and the evidence check section 13 | **Partly done (principle)** | No product found that markets this as its design stance, and no trial tests it. It is a design principle, not a differentiator. |

## E. Regulatory and safety boundary (brief)

- **EU MDR.** Software is a medical device when its intended purpose is medical (diagnosis, prediction, monitoring or treatment of a condition). Classification under Annex VIII Rule 11 depends on the impact of decisions based on it (information for diagnosis or therapy decisions, monitoring physiological processes, otherwise typically class I). MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (June 2025) adds examples including software with wearables or apps for mental health; general wellness software is excluded. https://gmpinsiders.com/mdcg-2019-11-rev1-mdr-ivdr/ (read; secondary summary, official text not read)
- **Practical line.** "Supports wellbeing, routines and mood" stays wellness; "detects relapse", "predicts depression", "treats depression" or "alerts your clinician that you are relapsing" makes it a device. UK MHRA guidance (published 3 February 2025, updated 29 July 2026) uses "to aid treatment of a mental health condition" as an example of a medical purpose. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-mental-health-technology-qualification-and-classification (read; UK, not EU)
- **For the pitch:** call the sensing a "check-in prompt" or "pattern noticed" for the user, not a diagnosis; the clinician view as a research or pilot feature under a clinic's supervision.
- **Crisis path, accepted practice.** A persistent help link plus reactive banners on very low mood or high screening scores; two or three curated services (emergency, 24/7, a suicide-specific line; in Finland this would need local verification); say plainly that the app is not a crisis service; test with vulnerable users; route any disclosure to trained humans; **do not rely on automated or AI responses alone** for crisis handling. https://8foldgovernance.com/expert-insights-crisis-signposting-in-digital-mental-health/ (read; consultancy summary of Samaritans guidance)
- **Why it matters:** a 2019 BMC Medicine review found only 5 of 69 depression and suicide-prevention apps offered all six evidence-based suicide-prevention strategies, and 6 listed a wrong crisis number. https://doaj.org/article/7e99b6d28dad4980a3ff43308c18693f (search only; page refused fetch)
