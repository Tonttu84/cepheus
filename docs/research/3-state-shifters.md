# What shifts mood state quickly, at what effort, and how to learn it per person

Checked 2026-11-13 with web search; builds on `../evidence-check.md` (sections 2, 3, 4, 7, 12) and does not repeat it. Sources were read at abstract or index-record level through search results; no full text was opened in this run, so effect sizes are as given in abstracts or in secondary citations, flagged where secondary. "No source found" means this run found nothing, not that nothing exists.

Effort scale (my judgement, not from the sources): **none** = passive, happens to you (light, ambient sound); **low** = one tap and under 5 minutes of following along; **medium** = leaving the house, phoning someone, 10–30 minutes of guided attention; **high** = sustained self-generated mental work (reappraisal, recalling and holding a memory, planning).

### Notes on the strongest items
- **Exercise.** Bourke et al. 2022 (J Affect Disord; PMID 35605707): 18 studies of acute physical activity in depression, SMD 0.585 (95% CI 0.456–0.714) for improved affect. Limitation stated by the authors: within-person pre–post only. Weinstein et al. 2024 (Psychosom Med; 103 studies, 4,671 participants) found substantial heterogeneity.
- **Mindful self-focus vs rumination.** Huffziger & Kühner (Behav Res Ther; depressed patients ~3.5 years after inpatient care, n=76): mindful self-focus and distraction both reduced induced sad mood relative to rumination. Kühner et al. 2023 (repeated ambulatory inductions over four days, remitted patients and controls): immediate benefit of mindful over ruminative focus on mood and cognition, stronger cognitive harm from rumination in patients, **no lasting effect over the day**. Implication: these responses are state shifters, not cures, and must be repeated.
- **Mood brightening** is the single most useful finding for the product: when a depressed person does reach a pleasant event, the payoff is not blunted, it is larger. The bottleneck is initiation (doctor's point 2).

## B. MBCT and its delivery

- **Components thought to carry the effect.** van der Velden et al. 2015 (Clin Psychol Rev 37:26; 23 studies): changes in mindfulness, rumination, worry, compassion or meta-awareness were linked to, predicted or mediated outcome in 12 studies; attention, memory specificity, self-discrepancy, emotional reactivity and momentary affect had preliminary support. Rumination findings were inconsistent across studies. Authors call for designs with more causal specificity.
- **Decentering via continued practice.** A 2019 J Consult Clin Psychol 24-month follow-up of remitted patients after CT or MBCT: post-treatment skill practice had no direct effect on relapse but lowered it indirectly by increasing decentering.
- **Home practice dose.** Parsons et al. 2017 (Behav Res Ther; 28 studies): small but significant link between home practice and outcome (r≈0.26, secondary citation). Strohmaier 2020 found no significant dose–response with *recommended* practice amount; Lloyd 2017 found 11 of 24 studies without the expected link.
- **Online / app MBCT.** Segal et al. 2020 (JAMA Psychiatry 77(6):563; n=460, Kaiser Permanente): online MBCT ("Mindful Mood Balance", 8 sessions + minimal coaching) plus usual care beat usual care on residual symptoms, remission and function. Lead author acknowledged that online dropout is higher than in-person. A web-based mindfulness review exists (Sevilla-Llewellyn-Jones 2018) but its dropout figures were not retrieved. **No meta-analysis of self-help MBCT dropout found.**
- **What a product can take without pretending to be the therapy:** (1) the 3-minute breathing space as a cue-triggered, rehearsed micro-practice (its acute effect is untested in isolation, but mindful self-focus vs rumination is supported in remitted patients, section A); (2) a personal relapse-signature / action plan, which MBCT includes; (3) choosing a pleasant or mastery activity after the breathing space (MBCT session 7 idea); (4) encouraging regular practice while well, since decentering appears to build with practice. Do not claim to deliver MBCT; refer to an 8-week course for that.

## C. CBASP

- **What it is.** McCullough's therapy for chronic depression. Core techniques: Situational Analysis (link one's own behaviour to actual outcomes in a specific episode — "perceived functionality"), the Interpersonal Discrimination Exercise (tell the therapist apart from hurtful significant others; aimed at people with early interpersonal trauma), and Contingent Personal Responsivity (disciplined personal involvement by the therapist).
- **Evidence.** Negt et al. 2016 (Brain Behav; 6 RCTs) concluded CBASP has supporting evidence in chronic depression: Keller 2000, CBASP + nefazodone beat either alone; Schramm 2011, CBASP ≈ IPT on observer ratings and better on self-report; Michalak 2015, CBASP > TAU. **But** the REVAMP trial (Kocsis 2009, Arch Gen Psychiatry; 491 non- or partial responders to medication): adding CBASP did **not** beat brief supportive therapy or optimised medication alone (phase-2 remission 15% overall, no group differences).
- **Relevance to post-TMS users.** TMS candidates are typically medication non-responders, i.e. close to the REVAMP population, where CBASP added nothing over medication. CBASP's strongest case is early-onset chronic depression with interpersonal trauma. Its Situational Analysis is the part that fits the doctor's point (5) and could be borrowed as a structured "what happened, what did I want, what did I get, what did I do" reflection *while well*; it is effortful (high effort) and therapist-dependent, so it is a poor fit for a dip.

## D. The doctor's mechanisms

### D1. Affect drives cognition; the reverse direction is weaker — **partly supported**
- Mood inductions reliably change affect (Joseph et al. 2020, Psychol Bull; 874 samples, N=53,509, δ=1.32), negative inductions roughly twice as strong as positive (secondary citation), and induced/dysphoric mood biases memory towards mood-congruent content (Faul & LaBar 2023 Psychol Rev; cue-word studies in dysphoria). Overgeneral memory persists in remission and predicts course.
- Reverse direction: cognitive bias modification, the cleanest "change cognition, see if mood follows" test, had small effects that became non-significant after outlier removal and publication-bias adjustment, mostly non-significant in patient samples (Cristea 2015, Br J Psychiatry, 49 RCTs). Concreteness training reduced rumination but did not beat relaxation on symptoms (evidence-check §2). CBT itself works, however, so "reasoning helps little" is too strong.
- Researcher-acceptable sentence: *"In depression, current mood reliably biases memory and judgement, while brief cognitive retraining shows weak effects on mood; this supports indirect, behavioural and attentional routes during a dip, but does not show that cognitive therapy is ineffective."*

### D2. Effortful self-regulation is impaired when unwell — **supported (with a nuance)**
- Snyder 2013 (Psychol Bull 139:81; 113 studies): MDD reliably associated with impaired executive function, d≈0.32–0.97. Effort-based decision-making reduced, g≈−0.28 (evidence-check §12b).
- Nuance: when *instructed*, depressed people can implement reappraisal and other strategies; the deficit is more in spontaneous selection and initiation (integrative review). EMA mood brightening shows reward is felt once reached.
- Sentence: *"Depression is associated with moderate executive-function deficits and a lower willingness to expend effort, while instructed strategy use is relatively preserved; tools should therefore remove the initiation step (prompt, one tap, pre-chosen action) rather than demand self-generated effort."*

### D3. Strong bad routines; replace habits by cue-based plans — **partly supported**
- Rumination as a habit: Watkins & Nolen-Hoeksema 2014 (J Abnorm Psychol 123:24) — repeated goal-discrepancy-triggered self-focus becomes context-cued and goal-independent. A counter-conditioning study with a concrete-thinking exercise reduced self-reported habitual rumination, but group change in rumination frequency was not significant.
- If-then plans: Toli & Webb 2015 (Br J Clin Psychol; 29 studies, clinical and analogue samples): d=0.99 on goal attainment in mental-health samples. MCII in depressed patients (n=47): 78.6% vs 31.6% reached their goal. A student thesis with depressive symptoms found no added effect.
- Habit formation takes long: median ≈66 days to automaticity plateau, range 18–254 (Lally 2010, secondary); Singh 2024 review: 59–66-day medians, 4–335 range, from only 4 studies.
- **No study found** that replaced self-blame or self-denial habits with cue-based plans in remitted depression specifically.
- Sentence: *"Rumination has a credible habit account and if-then planning robustly improves goal attainment including in clinical samples, but direct evidence that cue-based plans replace depressive habits in remitted people is lacking, and habit formation usually takes about two months."*

### D4. Skills learned while well are used in a later dip — **partly supported / largely untested**
- Skill practice after treatment lowered relapse indirectly through decentering (2019 JCCP). Qualitative CoBalT follow-up (n=20): people who saw CBT as learning kept using skills. Hollon et al. 2006 note patients may struggle to apply skills at the onset of an episode. Continuation CT focused on generalising skills lowered relapse (31%→18%, cited secondarily).
- Ambulatory work in remitted patients (Kühner 2023) shows mindful self-focus helps *in the moment* in daily life, but whether people deploy it unprompted during a real dip: **no source found**. MBCT's relapse effect (evidence-check §3) is indirect support.
- Sentence: *"Continued practice of skills after treatment is associated with lower relapse via decentering, but there is little direct evidence that people spontaneously deploy rehearsed skills at the onset of a dip, which is exactly the gap a prompt-at-the-right-time tool would test."*

## E. Learning what works for one person

### Methods and what they have shown
- **N-of-1 trials in depression.** Kronish et al. systematic review: few, small studies (treatment-resistant, inpatient, special populations); median 3 treatment pairs per person in N-of-1 reviews; authors conclude more research is needed. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5904006
- **Self-experiment apps.** Brown University mHealth N-of-1 study (2020): usable; enthusiasm followed users' *interpretation* of their results more than the results themselves — a warning that users will see effects in noise. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7336867/ . MINEs (up to 10,000 people, 18-day self-trials on stress/happiness) registered as completed; no published results found. StudyMe (feasibility only).
- **Micro-randomised trials.** Liao et al. 2016 (Stat Med 35:1944) give sample-size methods for proximal effects pooled across people (randomise at each decision point). Mental-health MRT results so far: proximal mood effects small and often CIs include zero (EMIcompass 2026), ACT app 2023 improved values-based behaviour. https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00238 ; https://doaj.org/article/4aef053a92b64372a56e7862fcbc91c2
- **JITAIs.** von Lützow et al. 2025 (BMJ Ment Health; 23 studies, n=2,563): small between-group effect g=0.15 (0.05–0.26), moderate–high risk of bias. https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/handle/uniba/116116
- **Bandits.** Deakin/Black Dog 2025 (JAMA Netw Open; n=1,282 students): contextual bandit assigned one of 4 two-week self-guided modules; physical activity and mindfulness best for severe distress, sleep hygiene and activity for mild, none distinguishable for moderate; authors estimate 25% fewer participants than a fixed trial. Assigns *between* people by context, not per-person learning. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2840791 . Kumar et al. 2024 (AAAI): Thompson-sampling text-message system, n=1,100; outcomes not retrieved. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18326
- **Within-person variability.** Within-person share of affect variance is large (NA 45–66%, PA 25–74%), mostly at the momentary level (Scott et al., coordinated analysis). https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/76445

### How many trials before a personal effect is distinguishable from noise
**No published figure for mood-response N-of-1 designs found.** A back-of-envelope calculation (mine, not from a source; two-sided α=0.05, 80% power, independent trials, outcome = pre-to-post change in a momentary mood rating, effect d expressed in units of that person's trial-to-trial SD of change):
- response vs. no response (two arms): n per arm ≈ 2 × 7.85 / d² → **d=1.0: 16; d=0.8: 25; d=0.5: 63**
- response vs. zero change (one arm, assuming no drift): n ≈ 7.85 / d² → d=0.8: 12; d=0.5: 31
- telling two *active* responses apart (difference d≈0.3): ≈175 per arm — practically impossible for one person.
- Autocorrelation and regression to the mean (prompting when mood is low guarantees some rebound) inflate these numbers; a no-response control arm is needed for the rebound.

Practical implication: per person, the app can realistically tell "this response works for me at all" for perhaps 2–4 candidates over 4–8 weeks of well-period trials (e.g. one trial a day), not rank ten options. Bayesian hierarchical models that start from the group prior (e.g. exercise and mindful self-focus have known acute effects) and shrink towards it, plus a bandit that stops exploring options that look poor, reduce the burden; this is standard methodology, but **no mental-health example of per-person (not per-context) learning with reported outcome was found**.
