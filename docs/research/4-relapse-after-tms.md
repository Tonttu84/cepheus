# Relapse after TMS in practice, and what the judging group works on

Checked 2026-11-13 with web search. This builds on `evidence-check.md` section 8 and does not repeat it. Unless noted, sources were read at abstract level. Full texts that were read: Senova 2019, Miljevic 2019, Mallon 2022, the Clinical TMS Society maintenance letter template, the HUS rTMS referral instruction, a 2025 Finnish lecture deck (TTL), and Woodham 2025 (results table only). "No source found" means this run found nothing, not that nothing exists.

## The problem as a TMS researcher would state it

"About half of acute rTMS responders lose response within a year. Maintenance and reintroduction work in uncontrolled data, but nobody knows **when** a given patient needs them. Today re-treatment is triggered by the patient getting worse enough to notice, get re-referred and wait. Measurement between visits is sparse or absent." A home product fits as the **outer, slow loop** (days to weeks): structured symptom monitoring that tells the clinic when to offer a booster (a maintenance cluster) before full relapse, plus support for the evidence-based non-TMS parts of relapse prevention. It does not replace the group's **inner, fast loop** (milliseconds to minutes), which adjusts stimulation from EEG during a clinic session.

## A. Relapse after successful TMS

### Rates and time course

- **Senova et al. 2019 (resolved from the full text).** The meta-analysis covers responders to an acute ("induction") rTMS course. Sustained response was:
  - **66.5%** at 3 months (95% CI 57.1–74.8; 18 studies, 732 patients)
  - **52.9%** at 6 months (95% CI 40.3–65.0; 18 studies, 695 patients)
  - **46.3%** at 12 months (95% CI 32.6–60.7; 9 studies, 247 patients)

  The abstract and the results section both give **46.3%**, so that is the figure to cite.
  - **Where 43.6% comes from.** Miljevic et al. 2019 cites Senova as reporting 43.6% at 12 months. That is a citation error. In Senova, 43.6% appears only as the **lower confidence limit** of the 3-month responder rate in studies without maintenance (56.1%, 95% CI 43.6–67.8).
  - **Maintenance subgroups.** At 3 months: 76.2% with maintenance vs 56.1% without. At 6 months: 61.1% vs 38.5%. The authors note that without maintenance the response rate fell by 31.4% from month 3 to month 6, and with maintenance by 19.8%.
  - **Caveat.** These are observational subgroups across studies, not randomised comparisons. The authors also call maintenance protocols "highly variable".
- **Janicak et al. 2010** (Brain Stimulation 3(4):187; open-label 24-week follow-up after the pivotal trial):
  - Partial responders were tapered off TMS onto antidepressant monotherapy.
  - 10 of 99 relapsed (Kaplan-Meier estimate 12.9%).
  - **38 of 99 (38%) met criteria for symptom worsening.** Of these, **32 (84.2%) regained benefit with reintroduced TMS.**
  - Worsening was a CGI-S rise of at least one point for 2 consecutive weeks (per search summaries of the paper).
  - So "relapse" figures depend on whether reintroduction was allowed: many people would have relapsed without rescue.
- **Dunner et al. 2014** (J Clin Psychiatry 75(12):1394; naturalistic, 257 patients, 42 US sites, 52 weeks):
  - Durability held over 12 months.
  - **93 patients (36.2%) received TMS reintroduction** after the taper month.
  - Search results (not the abstract) report 62.5% of responders and 70.5% of remitters keeping benefit at 12 months. Treat these as unverified.
  - Manufacturer-affiliated authors.
- **Accelerated protocols.** Geoly et al. 2025 (Brain Stimulation 18(3):875; SNT, 46 TRD patients, 5 days):
  - 70% remitted the week after treatment.
  - At **12 weeks only 33% (15/46) remained in remission**, i.e. 47% of the remitters.
  - The authors say comparisons with conventional rTMS should be made cautiously.
  - A University of Washington dissertation (secondary analysis, about 32 SAINT responders) reported a mean time to relapse of about 14 weeks without retreatment. This is weak evidence.
- **Theta burst vs standard rTMS.**
  - Acute efficacy is equivalent: a 2021 meta-analysis of 10 RCTs, 667 patients, found RR 1.02 for response.
  - **No source found** comparing long-term durability head to head.
  - A 2024 BMC Psychiatry meta-analysis of accelerated TMS reports that accelerated iTBS "only maintains" post-treatment effects, while accelerated rTMS showed continued improvement. Follow-up was short.

### Predictors of relapse

- **Miljevic et al. 2019** (J Affect Disord 256:317; systematic review, 18 studies):
  - Comorbid anxiety, degree of acute response and residual symptoms "may hold predictive potential", but the findings were "not sufficient to draw reliable conclusions".
  - **None of the studies was truly predictive.** None used baseline or end-of-treatment measures to predict relapse for an individual.
  - Mostly not predictive: age, sex, episode duration, medication, resistance level, rTMS frequency, and unilateral vs bilateral stimulation.
  - Maintenance reduced relapse in 3 studies and did not in 3.
  - The review typically defined remission as HDRS < 8 and relapse as HDRS ≥ 16.
- **Senova 2019 meta-regression.** A higher proportion of women predicted higher responder rates at 3 and 6 months, and maintenance at 3 months. Predictors were not tested at 12 months because there were too few studies.
- **SNT.** Prior non-response to rTMS predicted earlier relapse (8.5 vs about 14 weeks; dissertation, weak).

**Implication:** individual relapse prediction after TMS does not exist yet. That is an honest gap for a personal, data-driven model, but it also means no validated feature set is ready to use.

## B. What clinics do for maintenance

The evidence base is reviewed in **Wilson et al. 2022**, J Affect Disord 296:79, a systematic review of "preservation TMS": 30 sources, N = 1,494; 4 RCTs (1 sham-controlled), 14 open trials, 12 case series. Its findings:
- The evidence is low quality.
- Protocols vary.
- Preservation TMS "will likely be needed" for some patients.

The terms are:

| Strategy | What it is | Evidence found |
|---|---|---|
| **Taper** | Gradually fewer sessions after the acute course (for example 3/week, then 2, then 1) | Dalhuisen 2026: one RCT and several open studies, best when "symptom-guided" |
| **Scheduled (planned) maintenance** | Fixed-interval sessions, from twice-weekly to monthly, lasting 3 months to 2 years (Senova) | Mixed for single sessions |
| **Clustered maintenance** | About 5 sessions over 2–3 days, monthly | See the three studies below |
| **Reintroduction / rescue / retreatment** | A new course or cluster when symptoms return | Janicak 2010: 84% regained benefit; Dalhuisen 2026: effective in most prior responders, often with fewer sessions |

Clustered maintenance studies:
- **Fitzgerald 2013**, Brain Stimul 6:292, open label, n = 35, monthly 5 sessions over 2 days. 25 of 35 eventually relapsed, after a mean of 10.5 months. Before maintenance, their wellness periods had lasted less than 3 months.
- **Pridmore 2018**, Brain Stimul 11:1098, naturalistic, n = 39, monthly series of 5. Before each series 70% were not in remission; after it, 79% were in remission.
- **Wang 2017**, Transl Psychiatry 7:1292, assessor-blinded RCT, n = 281, monthly clusters of 5–10 sessions over 3–5 days, 12 months. Relapse or recurrence was **15.9%** with rTMS plus antidepressant, **24.2%** with rTMS alone and **44.4%** with antidepressant alone.
  - **Caveat:** these patients had remitted on **antidepressants**, not after a TMS course.

**Planned vs symptomatic reintroduction.** Senova found no significant difference between planned maintenance and symptom-triggered reintroduction (4 symptom-triggered studies vs 7 planned). This is the crux for a product: if symptom-triggered works as well as planned, **good symptom detection is what makes symptom-triggered care safe and cheaper**.

**Current guidance.**
- **Dalhuisen et al. 2026**, Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation 6:100210, the Dutch–Flemish consensus (CC BY; abstract only read):
  - Maintenance should be individualised.
  - Retreatment is more common in practice than tapering or maintenance; a survey of 11 Dutch institutions found tapering and maintenance used only "on a small scale".
  - Patient representatives asked for early discussion, flexible scheduling and **structured monitoring**.
- **Clinical TMS Society** (insurance-appeal template, 2025):
  - Defines relapse as symptoms that interfere with daily functioning for at least 2 weeks.
  - Recommends maintenance at "the minimum frequency of TMS that prevents the patient from having a recurrence".
  - States that insurers often require a patient to "fully relapse" before re-treatment is covered. Note that this is an advocacy document.
- **Reti et al. 2025**, Clin Neurophysiol 170 (NNDC consensus update, modified Delphi). Its maintenance recommendations were not read in this run.

**Medication and psychotherapy alongside.**
- In the large studies, patients continued or started antidepressants after TMS (Janicak: maintenance antidepressant monotherapy).
- **No source found** on structured psychotherapy or MBCT specifically after a TMS course. The general relapse-prevention evidence (MBCT and CBT) is in the evidence check, section 3, and section D below.

**Who notices, and what triggers the return.**
- In the trials, a symptom-scale threshold triggers it: a CGI-S rise held for 2 weeks (Janicak) or a HAMD threshold.
- In routine care, the patient or their treating psychiatrist notices worsening and arranges a new referral. HUS: "if needed, the course can be repeated with a new referral".
- UK NHS clinics phone patients about 7 days after the course and otherwise tell them to call if they get worse (Mallon 2022).
- So **detection is passive and patient-initiated**, and in insurance systems it is delayed until full relapse.

## C. The gap between visits

- **Follow-up practice.** Mallon et al. 2022, J Psychiatr Ment Health Nurs 29(3):463, interviewed 17 staff at NHS rTMS clinics:
  - Aftercare was typically a phone call after 7 days. Longer-term aftercare varied.
  - Patients were told they could contact the clinic at any time.
  - Clinics checked progress after the course to see whether "top up" treatment might be needed.
  - The offer of top-ups reassured patients that they were not "on their own".
- **What patients say about the end of treatment.** No patient-side qualitative study of the end of a TMS course was found. The evidence is second-hand:
  - In Mallon 2022, staff said some patients found ending difficult because they felt part of a treatment "community" after daily visits. Some were "tearful and upset to be leaving". One clinic ran a monthly support group so patients would not feel "chucked to the elements".
  - Mallon notes that qualitative rTMS research has focused on the treatment itself, "rather than the engagement, information, support, nursing care or follow-up package".
- **Remote monitoring after TMS.**
  - **NCT06145594** (Butler Hospital, USA) is the closest existing study to this product:
    - Randomised, open-label, n = 30, 1 year.
    - TMS responders answer a **weekly PHQ-9 on their smartphone**.
    - Software compares each score with a **personalised target**. When it rises past a threshold, a **5-session maintenance cluster over 3–5 days** is offered.
    - The comparison arm gets monitoring only.
    - The primary outcome is weeks in the response range (PHQ-9 ≤ target + 4).
    - Status: active, not recruiting; primary completion was estimated for September 2025. **No results found.**
  - **No published study found** that used wearables or passive sensing after a TMS course.
- **Home neuromodulation (tDCS)** is the neighbouring idea:
  - **Woodham et al. 2025**, Nat Med 31:87; fully remote, sham-controlled, n = 174, 10 weeks, remotely supervised; funded by the device maker Flow Neuroscience:
    - HDRS improvement was 9.41 points with active tDCS vs 7.14 with sham (p = 0.012).
    - HDRS remission was **44.9% vs 21.8%**. The 57.5% vs 29.4% figure seen elsewhere is MADRS remission.
    - Treatment-resistant patients were excluded (per the Finnish lecture deck).
    - There are no durability data after stopping.
  - **Borrione et al. 2024**, JAMA Psychiatry 81(4):329; n = 210, home tDCS **without** close supervision, with or without a digital psychological intervention: **not superior to sham** (all d ≤ 0.25, not significant).
  - Net: home tDCS is contested, and supervision may matter.
  - **No source found** for tDCS used as maintenance after TMS.

## D. Finland

- **Käypä hoito: depression** (current version published 25 June 2026, per kaypahoito.fi/hoi50023):
  - TMS is an effective acute treatment for depression and for drug-resistant depression (both grade A). The strongest evidence is for high-frequency stimulation of the left DLPFC. The main limits are access and equipment cost.
  - In young people with severe, treatment-resistant depression, TMS is "probably" effective (grade C).
  - **No mention found of maintenance TMS, repeat courses or home tDCS.** About a quarter of the page (the references section) was not read.
  - Relapse prevention:
    - Continue antidepressants for about 6 months after becoming symptom-free.
    - Consider long-term maintenance after a third episode (grade A).
    - MBCT for people with three or more episodes (grade A).
    - CBT or IPT maintenance therapy (grade A).
    - Maintenance ECT together with antidepressants (grade A).
    - At least yearly follow-up even when symptom-free.
- **Provision:**
  - **HUS** gives rTMS in neuromodulation units (Aurora, Psychiatry Centre, Peijas, Jorvi in Espoo). Indication: acute moderate or severe depression after two failed antidepressants, or when medication, psychosocial treatment or ECT are not suitable. The course is about 20 sessions on 3–5 days a week over 4–5 weeks. The effect "lasts months in most patients", and the course can be repeated **with a new referral** (HUS referral instruction, folder dated 2020; no maintenance programme is described).
  - **Scale.** An Aalto news article (13 Oct 2026) estimates 10,000–20,000 people with treatment-resistant depression in Finland each year, while **fewer than 800 received TMS in 2022**.
  - **Private clinics** exist; for example, the medical director of Clin Oy lectured on neuromodulation at TTL in May 2025. **No source found** on private prices or Kela reimbursement.
  - HUS also runs a TMS study comparing guideline-based and individually targeted TMS (HUS flyer, Dec 2023).

## E. The research group

This section uses only official Aalto pages and publications.

- **Group page** (Aalto NBE, TMS group). The page lists Pantelis Lioumis as Senior Scientist and Risto Ilmoniemi as Senior Advisor; the October 2026 news piece calls Lioumis the group's leader. Themes:
  - **Multi-locus TMS (mTMS):** a set of coils that moves the stimulation target electronically, without moving the coil. A five-coil device exists, and a commercial version is expected in 2027 from the spin-off Cortisys Oy.
  - Coil design.
  - **Closed-loop control** of target and timing from EEG or EMG feedback.
  - **TMS–EEG** signal processing: artefact rejection, source localisation, connectivity.
  - Navigated TMS for presurgical mapping.
  - rTMS for depression, pain, tinnitus and stroke.
  - Collaboration with HUS BioMag Laboratory.
- **ConnectToBrain.** An ERC Synergy grant (€10 M, grant 810377) with Aalto, Tübingen and Chieti-Pescara. Its aim is non-invasive, therapeutic, **closed-loop** stimulation for brain network disorders: a multi-coil helmet with integrated EEG and algorithms that adjust stimulation automatically. Targets include depression and stroke (Aalto news, 2021; project page).
- **What "closed-loop" means in their work.** Tervo et al. 2022, Brain Stimul 15(2):523 (with Lioumis and Ilmoniemi):
  - An algorithm reads **single-trial TMS-evoked EEG responses** and electronically adjusts the **stimulus orientation** with mTMS to maximise the response.
  - It gets close to the optimum within a few tens of pulses (about 1–2 minutes), tested in 6 healthy volunteers.
  - Here **closed-loop is a millisecond-to-minute control loop inside one session.** The sensed signal is the brain's own electrophysiological response to each pulse. The controlled variables are pulse location, orientation, intensity and timing.
- **How that differs from the challenge.** The challenge's "sense, respond, learn" is a **days-to-weeks behavioural loop** using self-report or wearables. Calling a mood app "closed-loop" in front of this group will sound loose. Better phrasing: "a slow, outer measurement loop that decides **when** to stimulate, complementing your fast inner loop that decides **how**."
- **Honest connection points:**
  1. **Personalisation and biomarkers between sessions.** Per Aalto news (Oct 2026), the group's personalisation research looks at "psychological and psychophysiological signs" that might show treatment effects **between sessions, before symptoms change**. Daily home measurements during and after the course could supply such a longitudinal outcome signal.
  2. **Access.** The group's stated motivation is that very few eligible Finns get TMS, and that automation can make it cheaper. Remote follow-up that reserves clinic time for patients who are actually worsening fits that goal.
  3. **Durability of mTMS or personalised TMS.** Any new protocol from the group will need follow-up data, and a home monitoring tool is cheap infrastructure for measuring durability.
  4. **Not a connection:** the product cannot do TMS–EEG or stimulation at home. Do not claim it.

## F. Questions for the mentors (each would change what to build)

1. "When a responder starts to worsen, would your clinic rather offer a **planned maintenance schedule or symptom-triggered reintroduction**, and is there capacity to give a 5-session cluster at short notice?" If neither, the product becomes self-management and alerting, not scheduling.
2. "Which **measure and threshold** would you trust to call back a patient: a weekly PHQ-9 or MADRS-S rise against the patient's own baseline (as in NCT06145594), or something else? And who in the HUS pathway would receive the alert, given that re-treatment needs a new referral?"
3. "Are you looking for between-session markers of treatment effect for your personalisation work? Would **daily self-report and sleep or activity data during the acute course** be useful to you, or only after it?"
4. "Is there any interest in **home tDCS as a bridge or maintenance** option, given Woodham 2025 vs Borrione 2024, or should home 'response' stay behavioural (activation, sleep, MBCT-style)?"
5. "What outcome would convince you a pilot worked: weeks in response over 12 months, time to relapse, fewer full relapses, or clinic visits saved?"

## Glossary

Use these terms correctly in front of TMS researchers.

- **rTMS**: repetitive TMS. **HF-rTMS**: high-frequency (about 10 Hz) stimulation, usually to the left **DLPFC** (dorsolateral prefrontal cortex).
- **iTBS**: intermittent theta burst stimulation, bursts of 50 Hz pulses repeated at 5 Hz, a few minutes per session. **cTBS**: the continuous, inhibitory form.
- **Accelerated TMS / SNT (formerly SAINT)**: several sessions a day over a few days. SNT is 10 iTBS sessions a day for 5 days, fMRI-targeted.
- **Acute course / induction course**: the initial daily course, typically 20–30 sessions.
- **Response**: at least a 50% drop on a depression scale (HAMD/HDRS or MADRS). **Remission**: below a cut-off, for example HDRS ≤ 7 (or < 8). These are not the same thing: "half stay responders" ≠ "half stay well".
- **Relapse vs recurrence**: relapse is the return of the same episode, recurrence is a new episode. Under the usual convention (not sourced in this run) the boundary is about 6 months of sustained remission, called recovery. Many TMS papers use "relapse" loosely.
- **Durability**: how long the benefit lasts after the course ends.
- **Continuation / maintenance / taper / cluster (massed) maintenance / reintroduction (rescue, retreatment) / preservation TMS**: see section B. "Preservation" is the umbrella term proposed by Wilson 2022.
- **TRD**: treatment-resistant depression, usually defined as at least 2 failed adequate antidepressant trials.
- **Motor threshold (MT, rMT)**: the intensity that produces a motor response; stimulation dose is set as a percentage of it.
- **Neuronavigation / navigated TMS**: MRI-guided coil placement.
- **mTMS**: multi-locus TMS (Aalto); the target moves electronically.
- **TMS–EEG / TEP**: EEG recorded during TMS; TEP is the TMS-evoked potential.
- **Closed-loop (in this group's sense)**: real-time adjustment of stimulation from the brain's measured response, such as EEG, EMG or brain state. Do not use it for a weekly app loop without qualifying it.
- **Sham-controlled vs open-label**: most maintenance evidence is open-label.
- **sgACC**: subgenual anterior cingulate cortex; its functional anticorrelation with the DLPFC target relates to outcome (see the evidence check).
